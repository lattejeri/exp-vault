## The context

*   Recently placed into the multi-year QUT Mobile App 2.0 project just four months before its scheduled closure
*   Stepped into the role of Product Owner
*   Needed to get up to speed incredibly fast on a completely new technology stack
*   Collaborated with a highly talented team of specialist application developers and product designers to successfully "land the plane."

## **Managing expectations is just as important as the final delivery**

Transitioning a million-dollar project into day-to-day operations requires excellent stakeholder management. It is crucial to ensure both internal teams and external business partners understand that the team's delivery scale will naturally reduce once the project shifts into maintenance mode. 

## **A clear roadmap provides much-needed stability for a lost team**

Because I joined the project late in its lifecycle, the team was seeking direction after some key members had moved on. We held prioritization workshops to clearly define what could realistically be achieved, getting Steering Group endorsement to finish in-scope features to a high standard. We then took the out-of-scope items and turned them into a solid, structured roadmap for the post-project operational team. 

## **Keeping internal team members happy is just as important as business stakeholders**

Project closure means carefully winding down the contingent labor force while ensuring your permanent team stays engaged. I made sure to meet with team members individually to genuinely listen to their concerns and follow up on their queries, even when the answers weren't easy. This hands-on approach helped preserve a strong team culture, carrying continuous release cycles, agile rituals, and a proactive mindset right over into operations. 

## **The project isn't truly closed until you prove its value**

Finally, a successful wrap-up means definitively proving the project's return on investment. You can achieve this by mapping the post-launch usage analytics directly back to your initially stated project benefits. It’s the absolute best way to show the true, measurable impact of all that hard work.
